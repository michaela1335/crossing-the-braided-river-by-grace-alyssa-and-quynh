TARAPIROHE Chronicle of the Braided River

Across New Zealand’s braided rivers, fewer than 10,000 Tarapirohe survive. Bare river shingle leaves their speckled eggs exposed to introduced predators—feral cats, stoats, hedgehogs, and gulls—while lupins, gorse, and dry channels threaten their sanctuary islands.

Scroll Controls:
• Move: WASD keys, Virtual Joystick, or Click on the river scroll
• Collect Eggs: Hold Q (or Collect button) over bare river stones
• Finish Crossing: Press Space (or Land button) at the Torii Sanctuary Island, then hold E to secure eggs in the nest!
📜 River Ecology Scroll 5 Chapters
  • I. Only 5,000–10,000 Tarapirohe are left in the world.
  • II. They lay eggs right on bare river stones, making them easy to find and eat.
  • III. One feral cat can wipe out almost an entire nesting colony in one night.
  • IV. Predators like stoats are strong swimmers that cross river channels.
  • V. Lupin and gorse overgrow bare gravel, hiding predators near nests.
  • VI. Dams & irrigation dry up channels, letting predators walk to islands.
